Why does OpenSSH draw this?

    
When you run ssh-keygen -lv, OpenSSH feeds every byte of the fingerprint's digest to a
    drunken bishop. The bishop starts at the center of a 17×9 board and, for each 2-bit pair
    (read LSB-first), steps diagonally: bit 0 → right/left, bit 1 → down/up. Steps off the board
    are clamped to the edge. Every visited square accumulates a count, rendered with this ladder:

    
" . o + = * B O X @ % & # / ^" — then S marks the start, E the final square.

    
Same key ⇒ same walk ⇒ same picture. That's the trick: you can recognize a server's key at a glance
    without comparing hex strings character by character. BISHOPART reproduces the exact OpenSSH algorithm
    (verified byte-for-byte against ssh-keygen -lv) and lets you watch the walk itself.
